# 5. Results and Analysis (Text Version)

All numbers in this section are aligned to the unified test set, `data/test.conll` (98 English tokens, 7 POS labels with non-zero support). Final values are fixed in `report/data/test_metrics_snapshot.json`, and figures are generated by `report/generate_readme_performance_figures.py` and `report/generate_local_supplement_figures.py`.

## 5.1 Overall Performance Comparison

Table 5-1 reports Accuracy and weighted Precision / Recall / F1 for all four systems.

**Table 5-1. Overall performance on English-token POS tagging (test set).**

| System | Accuracy | Precision (weighted) | Recall (weighted) | F1 (weighted) |
|--------|----------|----------------------|-------------------|---------------|
| PyCantonese baseline | 0.479 | 0.344 | 0.275 | 0.278 |
| + Rule-based patch | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| BiLSTM-CRF (5-seed ensemble) | 0.806 | 0.794 | 0.806 | 0.793 |
| mBERT fine-tune | 0.908 | 0.849 | 0.816 | 0.853 |

**Key observations**

1. The rule-based system reaches perfect performance on the current test split.
2. Among neural methods, mBERT outperforms BiLSTM-CRF in weighted F1 (`0.853` vs `0.793`).
3. BiLSTM-CRF still provides clear gains over the baseline.
4. PyCantonese remains a weak baseline in this code-switching setting.

Related figures: `report/figures/readme_test_overall_accuracy_f1.png`, `report/figures/readme_improvement_vs_baseline.png`, and `report/figures/readme_test_macro_f1_four_models.png`.

## 5.2 Per-class F1 Analysis

Per-label details are shown in `report/data/test_metrics_snapshot.json` and `report/figures/local_per_label_prf_rule_vs_bilstm.png`.

- **PyCantonese:** weak on `VERB` and `PROPN`; frequent noun/proper-noun bias remains visible.
- **Rule-based:** perfect per-label F1 on this test split; zero confusion in the test confusion matrix.
- **BiLSTM-CRF:** strongest on major classes (`NOUN`, `PROPN`, `VERB`), but still suffers from confusion among these categories.
- **mBERT:** highest overall neural score; still leaves room for recall improvement on difficult tokens.

Confusion matrices are available in:

- `report/figures/local_confusion_bilstm_ensemble_test.png`

## 5.3 Qualitative Examples

The following examples are from `data/test.conll` and aligned with:
- `models/mbert/results/test_pred.conll`
- `models/bilstm_crf/results/ensemble_test_pred.conll`
- PyCantonese outputs under the same evaluation protocol.

**Table 5-2. Qualitative comparison on selected examples.**

| Sentence snippet | Word | Gold | PyCantonese | BiLSTM | mBERT |
|------------------|------|------|-------------|--------|-------|
| ...怕你 **miss** 了 | miss | VERB | NOUN | VERB | VERB |
| ...你们 **really** made our day! | really | ADV | NOUN | VERB | ADJ |
| ...一百個 **Like** 讚你哋... (nominal use) | Like | NOUN | PROPN | VERB | VERB |

**Discussion**

1. For **miss**, both neural systems match the gold label, while the baseline defaults to `NOUN`.
2. For **really**, both baseline and neural models still show errors on adverb-like usage.
3. For **Like** in a nominal context, both neural models predict `VERB`, showing remaining context disambiguation challenges.
